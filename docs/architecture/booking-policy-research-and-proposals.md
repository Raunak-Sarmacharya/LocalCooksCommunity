# Booking policy research and provisional recommendations

2026-10-11. **Draft for owner review, not approved policy or final architecture.** Owner expressly asked for proposals after the current-state questions; exact cancellation/response thresholds remain unselected. [Current state](booking-current-state.md) stays separate. E = current official external evidence; F owner context; R repository; I inference; J recommendation/product judgment; Q unresolved. No legal entitlement is inferred from another marketplace's terms.

## Recommendation in plain language [J]

Require final kitchen access approval and valid mandatory credentials before allowing a paid booking request. A chef's ordinary cancellation should follow the terms shown when they booked, using the time the platform receives the cancellation, without a manager's delayed response removing an earned refund. If a kitchen or Local Cooks cannot supply an unused confirmed booking, return the customer's entire affected payment, including platform fee and relevant tax. Allocate Stripe's nonreturnable cost internally; do not deduct it from a blameless customer.

For a startup, prefer one clear standard chef-cancellation schedule at launch, with a full-refund window and plainly stated later treatment; choose actual cutoff/late refund levels only after booking lead-time, resale and margin evidence and owner approval. Do not launch a catalogue of brand-named policies or a generic fault/rules engine. [J/Q]

These sources offer contradictory timing/fee/support choices, not one unanimous top-company policy. Recommendations below are J based on Local Cooks' specific R risks and startup constraints, not claims that every marketplace uses them.

## P1: final approval and evidence validity [J]

Propose a single customer-visible **Approved for this kitchen/location** state after required Local Cooks checks and the manager's final review. Intermediate approval means **Continue onboarding**, never approved-to-book. No paid checkout until final access and required credentials are valid. Known-client reuse can reduce repeated data entry/review while preserving required evidence, reviewer identity and explicit documented exemptions.

At request, check present validity and whether requirements cover the intended session. If validity will lapse before use, explain the renewal requirement; do not imply an unconditional confirmed right of access without required evidence. Exact requirement-by-requirement validity, business/location scope and renewal/waiver authority remain Q. A blanket rule that every document can never expire after booking would overreach.

If requirements expire after confirmation: stop new requests where required; notify chef and manager; review upcoming session safety/access promptly. Do not silently delete a paid booking, remove inventory or charge a penalty. Session suspension, renewal, cancellation attribution and refunds require documented reason and review when disputed. A kitchen/platform administrative mistake is not automatically a chef-caused failure. Evidence: tier2 self-grant versus tier3 UI R, expiry gate missing R, Food Corridor configured current-document control E.

## P2: timely chef cancellation is effective without a manager veto [J]

For ordinary customer cancellation, use the platform-recorded submission time against the saved booking policy and exact kitchen-local deadline. If the submission earns a refund under the policy, a later manager response or failed reminder must not remove it. Manager may challenge factual exceptions/used resources with evidence, not veto a routine eligible cancellation. Outside standard refund entitlement, offer a clearly separate discretionary refund request.

Display and confirm a quote before action: affected session/items, amount paid, amount to refund, retained line items, policy version and timestamp; then receipt stating cancellation recorded and financial processing state. An email receipt arriving late is not the cancellation time. Button actor is not fault: kitchen asking chef to cancel must not reclassify failure as voluntary chef cancellation. Evidence: current acceptance uses decision time R; formal platform cancellation E; recommendation improves predictability I/J.

If application/payment is only authorized before confirmation: cancel the request and release the hold, no capture/processing deduction. If a legacy booking has already captured money before confirmation, reconcile and return the amount owed; do not apply post-confirmation penalty merely because a legacy path differs. This proposal requires later design/recovery work; no code changed.

## P3: kitchen/platform failure — full affected refund, internal cost ownership [J]

For unused confirmed time the kitchen cannot provide, refund the customer's affected kitchen price, unused committed add-ons, relevant tax and platform fee. Same approach for duplicate allocation or platform-caused inability to honour the booking. Customer is not required to accept credit/reschedule. Offer alternatives only within real availability; do not promise replacement venue or consequential-loss coverage a startup cannot support.

**Stripe cost:** customer-paid amount, processor expense and manager settlement are different quantities. Stripe says original processing fees are not returned, and separate-charge/transfer refunds debit the platform; recovering connected funds requires transfer reversal. [Stripe refund/Connect behavior](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds). [E] Paying a full refund simply means kitchen/platform funds the sunk fee; it does not mean Stripe refunds its expense.

Example [illustrative, not actual fee rate]: chef paid $100; Stripe kept $3; available net $97. Return $100 to the chef and account for $3 as kitchen/platform expense. Calling a $97 return “full refund” would be misleading. Actual fees, balances and reversal availability must be verified, not estimated as provider truth.

At launch, propose Local Cooks executes the customer remedy and initially absorbs modest processor losses under a monitored loss budget, avoiding delayed refunds while arguing fault or collecting tiny debts. Kitchen-caused actual processor cost can later be assigned/recovered under an agreed contract and existing financial records; no arbitrary cancellation fine or unapproved external debit. Platform commission must also be returned for platform/kitchen failure. [J/Q]

This refines the owner's hypothesis: responsibility determines internal cost and consequences, but **objective failure to deliver should establish the customer remedy without waiting for a blame investigation**. Kitchen-cancels-before-use is a simple case; inaccessible kitchen, unsafe equipment, disputed credentials and weather are not reliably classified by whoever clicked Cancel.

## P4: voluntary chef cancellation — two viable fee approaches [J]

**Preferred:** inside the advertised full-refund window, return the entire affected customer payment including platform fee and applicable tax; platform absorbs sunk Stripe expense as a measured cost of trust. Outside it, apply the saved, clearly quoted partial/no-refund schedule. Start with one standard schedule, not multiple kitchen-configurable policy families. Do not promise “full” while deducting hidden processing costs.

**Viable alternative:** eligible voluntary chef cancellation returns the advertised refundable components minus the verified nonreturnable processor cost, subject to applicable law/contracts. This can preserve margin, but describe it as **refund less processing cost**, show actual deduction before action and before checkout, cap it at actual allocated cost, and do not also duplicate that cost in a fee deduction. Never apply it to kitchen/platform failure. This is a policy option, not approved. Tax allocation and partial/shared-PI fee basis need later financial design.

The preferred option is simpler for customers and support. The alternative is slightly simpler for current net-refund source behavior but requires more careful disclosure, allocation and dispute handling. Margin evidence should choose between them; “Stripe keeps its fee” alone does not decide customer refund amount. No specific cutoff, retention percentage or late grace period proposed as settled.

## Exceptions and startup controls [J/Q]

| Control | Risk / permanence | Simplest safe operating approach / cost | Relaxation evidence / removal path |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final approval plus explicit evidence scope | Unsafe/unapproved access; permanent eligibility invariant | Existing tiers/requirements and reviewer audit, clear final state; renewal-review workload | Evidence reuse may reduce repetition after equivalent requirements proven; keep final approval invariant |
| Timely submission determines entitlement | Manager delay defeats right; permanent policy invariant | Recorded cancellation receipt and saved terms; managers review factual exceptions only | No removal; improve automation after state/recovery tests |
| Full affected refund on supply failure | Customer pays for unavailable service; permanent promised remedy | Operations can use existing provider/ledger review; retain original identity, avoid fresh refunds after uncertainty | Automate routine cases after scoped refund/reversal evidence; keep invariant |
| Small monitored processor-loss allowance | Premature blame/debt workflow and refund delay; temporary startup safeguard | Platform absorbs under owner-approved monetary ceiling; operational review when ceiling reached, never silently deny already-promised remedy | Actual loss/GMV, cancellation causes, repeat abuse and margins justify contract-backed recovery; no invented ceiling here |
| Admin review for disputed/partial-use cases | Incorrect attribution, unsafe automatic penalties; temporary safeguard | Few reason categories, evidence, timestamps and named owner; one case record, no fault engine | Automation only for repeatedly objective cases with low reversal/appeal error; retain manual appeal |
| No host-induced “chef cancellation” | Fee-shifting/manipulation; permanent integrity rule | Distinct reason/cause; support correction with history; moderate case workload | Better UX can reduce support, preserve correction/audit |
| Clear original policy/change history | Retroactive disadvantage; permanent trust invariant | Use existing saved terms as evidence; full policy version/snapshot remains architecture hypothesis to evaluate | New data design later; missing old evidence requires review, not fabricated policy |
| Restricted real diagnostics | Accidental users/payments/shared storage effects; permanent boundary | Mock until exact downstream targets/recipients verified; small local test cost | Verified dedicated test resources allow bounded end-to-end testing, not removal of boundary |

Used sessions/occupied storage/consumed resources require scoped remedies, not automatic refund of unrelated delivered value. No automatic charge or cancellation penalty for a disputed safety issue or expired evidence without causal review. Emergency/force-majeure allocation, law, tax adjustments, fraud/repeat cancellation controls and contractual processing-cost recovery remain Q; platform must not advertise certainty it cannot fulfil.

## Adversarial reconciliation before presenting the draft

| Review finding | Disposition / revision |
|---|---|
| R1 refund liquidity and early transfers | **Accept.** Current transfer can occur near capture, not after use; operational payout schedule unknown. A promise needs funded platform liquidity for the full unrecovered customer liability, not only a tiny fee budget. Customer remedy must not depend on successful manager reversal. Contract-backed future set-off/invoice/reserve or transfer-timing alternatives require commercial/legal/provider analysis in the gated architecture phase; none implemented/selected. Return of commission is foregone platform revenue, not a second external Stripe processing expense. |
| R2 capture versus refund cost | **Accept.** Only uncaptured release avoids original captured-payment refund cost in the inspected standard flow. Prefer honestly funded gross refund after capture within a bounded saved window; alternative disclosed net refund remains for voluntary chef cancellations only. Observed fee-free reversal exceptions are not a budgeting guarantee. Never retroactively shrink an existing promise when a cost metric worsens. |
| R3 validity reference and existing tier2 grants | **Accept with qualification.** At request and confirmation, required evidence should cover session end when applicable. Requirements without expiry are not forced into this rule; lawful renewal/conditional booking may need explicit policy. Existing self-granted entitlements/bookings need an admin review plan, not silent revocation or invented grandfathered safety clearance. Kitchen licence revocation is a supply-failure/safety case, not chef fault. |
| R4 effective cancellation and financial failure | **Accept.** Ordinary policy cancellation becomes effective on committed submission, not later manager acceptance. Refund/reversal pending/failure must not reinstate the booking. Occupied storage removal stays separately controlled. |
| R5 kitchen cancellation and blame | **Accept default customer remedy; narrow suggested claim reuse.** A manager cannot turn an ordinary supply failure into fee retention by claiming chef fault. Disputed fraud/safety/partial-use cases require a recorded admin decision. Existing damage claims are not a general authorization to charge cancellation losses or processor fees. No new automatic card debit proposed. |
| R6 request caps and cancellation counts | **Accept observability/review, qualify cost.** Existing records can support counts/manual repeat-abuse review without new infrastructure, but implementation and support still cost effort. Any cap/threshold needs owner approval and must allow legitimate recurring use; no fines or retroactive fee shifts. |
| R7 disputed refunds/no-fault/weather interim default | **Accept dispute double-refund protection and named interim owner; reject blanket weather rule.** Ordinary weather, extreme disruption, kitchen utility/access failure and chef travel issues are not equivalent. Preserve recorded entitlement and flag unreconciled money; stop any unstarted transfer where possible, but do not assume already-transferred funds can be frozen. No-fault fee allocation and affected-unused-value remedy require defined event/evidence rules; [Airbnb's disruptive-events policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1320) expressly excludes many ordinary travel/weather circumstances. |
| R8 no capture after start | **Accept J proposed invariant.** Require confirmation before use and a visible response deadline bounded by session start and actual provider validity; no numeric SLA chosen. Startup exception for an already-started unconfirmed paid session is review/recovery, not ordinary late capture. |
