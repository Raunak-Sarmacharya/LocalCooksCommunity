# LocalCooks booking standards: phase 0 research

**Research/access date: 2026-10-11.** Every linked source below was accessed for this research on that date. Sources are official company documentation, government publications, or technical standards. Older announcements are identified where they establish a historical change; they are not represented as newly published policies. Public sources describe specific products and regions, not necessarily every customer configuration.

**Scope:** Research and recommendations only. No LocalCooks services, databases, Stripe accounts, or application code were accessed. The LocalCooks configuration described here comes from the task brief and has not been audited. Recommendations are proposed standards, not statements about the current implementation or legal/accounting advice.

**Recommended direction:** Keep manager review as the default while kitchen eligibility, equipment conflicts, and onboarding require judgment. Make approval, payment, and confirmation separate, explicit facts. Choose one documented pending-payment model; snapshot the accepted commercial terms; show an attainable total price; express every booking deadline in the kitchen's local time; and reconcile refunds with manager transfers. Integrate TOUR as a related appointment and onboarding step, rather than treating a completed visit as permission to cook.

## 1. Booking model, pending payment, response deadlines, and expiry

### What top companies do

- **Airbnb:** Instant Book confirms immediately; requests give the host 24 hours. Its card-authorization help describes a full-price hold that becomes a charge after acceptance; rejection or expiry releases the hold, and banks can take up to seven business days to reflect release. Other Airbnb help uses refund wording for pending payments, so the customer-facing explanation must reflect the actual payment method and flow. A hold is not a settled charge. [Reservation statuses](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/234), [authorization holds](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3549), [pending reservations](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2810).
- **Peerspace:** Offers requests and Instant Book. Its current host help says **two business days** to accept or decline, followed by automatic expiry. Acceptance charges the guest; decline releases the authorization. Hosts receive email/text notifications containing booking details and expected payout. This supersedes the older 2022 article's 48-hour description; business days and elapsed hours are different commitments. [Current booking-request help](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10119524-what-happens-when-i-receive-a-booking-request), [booking/payment overview](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10546795-peerspace-101-getting-started), [older article](https://www.peerspace.com/resources/accepting-booking-requests/).
- **Vrbo:** Instant Booking and **24-hour Review** coexist. Review requests hold calendar dates; acceptance charges the card and sends confirmation, decline releases the calendar hold, and no response automatically declines the request after 24 hours. The cited page does not establish the precise pending **card** authorization mechanism; a calendar hold must not be described as a card hold without evidence. [Accepting booking requests](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-accept-a-booking-request), [Instant Booking](https://help.vrbo.com/category/undefined/articles/What-is-Instant-Booking).
- **Booking.com:** Its original **INQUIRY** request-to-book mode gives the property 24 hours to respond, then the guest 24 hours to finish booking. No reservation exists merely because the inquiry was approved. Its newer, feature-gated **RtB 2.0** mode instead pre-authorizes the full stay and holds inventory at request time; property approval confirms and settles payment without a second guest step. Rejection/expiry/failure must release inventory. These are distinct documented versions, not evidence that all Booking.com bookings follow one model. [RtB overview](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/request-to-book/overview), [RtB 2.0](https://developers.booking.com/connectivity/docs/request-to-book/rtb-2.0/overview).
- **OpenTable/Resy:** Restaurant reservations illustrate immediate reservation plus restaurant-specific card guarantees, deposits, or prepaid experiences. A restaurant can charge a disclosed late-cancellation/no-show fee; a reservation is not universally a prepaid purchase. Resy's Global Dining Access guarantee rules are program-specific. [OpenTable payment/cancellation policies](https://help.opentable.com/s/article/What-are-cancellation-no-show-and-deposit-policies?language=en_US), [Resy program-specific policy](https://helpdesk.resy.com/en_us/global-dining-access%3A-cancellation-policy-faq-BkzqbD7LO).
- **Turo:** Its June 2024 announcement confirms Book Instantly became standard for trips. Current refund help distinguishes an authorization from charging under the chosen payment option; cancellation before charging releases the hold. Do not copy an obsolete host-response workflow or assume every instant booking collects the full amount immediately. [2024 product update](https://turo.com/blog/news/unveiling-70-key-product-updates-this-summer/), [payment/refund timing](https://help.turo.com/refund-timing-rkyN44gEc).
- **Getaround:** Current international/European help distinguishes owner-reviewed requests, paid after acceptance, from instant Getaround Connect reservations. Its payment options can include payment/deposit authorization or eligible deferred payment with later authorization and automatic cancellation after unresolved payment failure. These are regional product rules; Getaround Connect is the car-access product, not Stripe Connect. No universal owner-response deadline was verified. [Booking methods](https://fr.getaround.com/help/articles/31eb65474631/intl), [payment options](https://fr.getaround.com/help/articles/877bf76d8fc6/intl).
- **Commercial kitchens:** The Food Corridor defaults to manager approval with an option to approve clients automatically. Its operational billing documentation describes usage charged after the booking and recurring storage billing, rather than proving an Airbnb-style checkout hold. The Kitchen Door, operated by The Food Corridor, recommends viewing a kitchen before booking unless already familiar, and places the kitchen hire agreement between host and client. [Food Corridor approvals](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1413916-how-can-i-approve-bookings), [billing](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1413985-how-does-billing-work), [Kitchen Door terms](https://www.thekitchendoor.com/tos).
- **Kitchen-platform evidence limits:** KitchenShare.ai markets hourly/day/week use with owner approval; One World KitchenShare in Indiana is a different business and describes application, contract/deposit, and onboarding before use, with requests seven days ahead and approval usually within 24 hours. CloudKitchens has a tour/sales funnel. Kitchen United's current public site emphasizes restaurant operations/takeout. No sufficiently documented current authorization, expiry, or cancellation workflow was found for CloudKitchens or Kitchen United; their marketing is not evidence of an hourly marketplace standard. [KitchenShare.ai](https://kitchenshare.ai/), [One World KitchenShare FAQ](https://www.kitchenshare.net/faq), [CloudKitchens tour](https://go.cloudkitchens.com/tour-a-kitchen-typeform-qq-nationwide), [Kitchen United](https://kitchenunited.com/).

**Stripe constraint:** Online card authorizations are commonly seven days, but network and transaction type matter: Visa merchant-initiated authorizations can be approximately five days, technically four days and 18 hours. Read the charge's actual `capture_before`; do not hard-code seven days. Checkout supports manual capture through `payment_intent_data[capture_method]=manual`. Expiry cancels an uncaptured PaymentIntent and releases the authorization. Extended authorizations can reach approximately 30 days only where eligible, with network/category/pricing restrictions; a kitchen must not be assumed to qualify as lodging. [Card holds/manual capture](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method), [extended authorization](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/extended-authorization?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted).

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

1. **Keep request-to-book by default.** Propose a **24 elapsed-hour** manager decision limit, with an exact visible expiry timestamp. This is a LocalCooks recommendation, not a kitchen-industry requirement. Enforce sufficient booking lead time for review, payment, and preparation; do not allow a request to remain pending into its intended start time.
2. **Select and name one payment flow.** For a simple approval-first design: `Requested → Approved, payment due → Confirmed, paid → In use → Completed`. Give approved requests a proposed maximum 24-hour payment window, shortened where the minimum lead-time rule requires it, and show the actual deadline. Declined, withdrawn, and expired requests release inventory automatically. Approval alone must not trigger a paid confirmation.
3. **If commitment at request time is necessary, use a clearly disclosed card authorization.** Authorize when requesting, capture only after manager approval, and treat authorization and settlement as separate states. Review expiry must precede the actual authorization expiry with a safety margin. A capture failure requires payment recovery, not a false confirmation. Do not keep an authorization until a cooking session weeks away, or enable a deadline-triggered capture that could charge an unapproved request. The Stripe sources above establish this alternative's constraints.
4. **Charge-then-refund is a separate product choice.** If chosen, disclose that money is collected immediately and automatically refund rejected/expired requests. Do not call the debit a hold or promise instant bank availability. Do not assess which flow the existing code uses without a separate authorized audit.
5. **Make expiry deterministic.** Keep distinct deadlines for manager response, payment, Checkout session, and authorization. After expiry, require a fresh request/quote rather than silently reviving an old one. Notify both parties, explain whether no payment occurred, a hold was released, or a refund was initiated, and display the bank-processing caveat only when applicable.
6. **Reserve actual resources.** Hold the kitchen and selected scarce equipment/storage capacity for a bounded period; atomically check conflicts at request, approval, and confirmation. Explicitly choose exclusive pending holds or competing requests with only one possible winner. A late approval, duplicate webhook, or payment arriving after capacity was released must never create two confirmed bookings. Resolve the paid-but-unavailable race through a documented refund/support path.
7. **Freeze the quote and terms before consent.** Capture currency, dates, resources, total, cancellation terms, and relevant tax treatment. A manager's material change requires a revised quote and renewed guest acceptance. For a genuinely zero-total booking, confirm through the same eligibility/inventory checks without fabricating a Stripe charge.
8. **Integrate TOUR as its own appointment.** Link tour and rental records to the same location/customer; distinguish tour requested/confirmed/completed from kitchen approval. Show prerequisites such as orientation or required documents separately. A tour may consume staff or kitchen capacity, but should block rental resources only where there is a real conflict. Offer a contextual booking link after the visit subject to CASL review in section 5.

### Later at scale

Offer manager-controlled Instant Book to eligible, previously onboarded renters; require reliable calendars and equipment capacity. Add recurring rentals, waitlists, quote amendments, expiry escalation, and measured decision times. Consider authorization-at-request only when reduced abandonment justifies payment complexity. Long lead-time bookings may need a scheduled future charge/deposit arrangement rather than longer card holds. Do not promise universal extended-authorization eligibility.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

- When does a binding hire agreement form: request, manager approval, guest payment, or final confirmation? Align cancellation rights and payment language with that answer.
- Is each arrangement a licence, rental, service, or another supply, and what kitchen licensing, insurance, food-safety, orientation, and occupancy permissions must precede use?
- Are deposits, immediate collection, saved-card future collection, and expiry/refund terms enforceable for business renters and any consumers? Which tour terms and injury/liability disclosures are needed?

## 2. Cancellation policies, refunds, exceptions, and versioning

### What top companies do

- **Airbnb:** Current short-stay options are broader than the old Flexible/Moderate/Strict shorthand. Flexible permits full cancellation refund until 24 hours before check-in; Moderate uses five days. Limited uses a 14-day full-refund window and Firm 30 days, with half-refund stages down to seven days. Strict is restricted to invited hosts. Standard short-stay policies allow cancellation within 24 hours of confirmation when confirmed at least seven days before check-in. Deadlines use listing local time. The headline accommodation refund is not a universal promise about Airbnb guest service fees. [Current policies](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/475). Host cancellation can bring monetary penalties, lost payout, calendar restrictions, and account consequences, with documented exceptions. [Host cancellation policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/990). Major Disruptive Events is a defined destination-event policy, not a general refund for any personal hardship. [Policy update](https://news.airbnb.com/an-update-on-our-major-disruptive-events-policy).
- **Peerspace:** Its August 2025 policy defines Very Flexible, Flexible, Standard 30, and Standard 90. For example, Flexible refunds fully until seven days, half of the booking price excluding fees between seven days and 24 hours, then nothing. Grace-period cancellation must be within 24 hours of confirmation and no later than 48 hours before use. Windows use the space's local time; nonconsecutive rental days are evaluated separately. Pre-use cleaning fees are refundable, and food/drink add-ons have separate treatment. The policy effective when booked or subsequently amended governs. Unexcused host cancellation produces a full guest refund and potential fines/restrictions; documented exceptional circumstances can excuse penalties. [Cancellation policy](https://www.peerspace.com/legal/terms/cancellation-policy).
- **Vrbo:** Offers several presets, including Moderate with full refund at least 30 days ahead and half between 14 and 30 days, and Strict with full refund at least 60 days ahead and none later. Its help specifies property-zone 11:59 PM cutoffs for the applicable last day; understand the distinct 24-hour wording of the Day-before option. Policy changes do not alter existing reservations. Full-refund eligibility includes the service fee; partial refunds do not generally return it. [Policy choices/versioning](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-the-cancellation-policy-options), [service fee](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/what-is-the-service-fee), [partial fee refunds](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/If-I-or-my-traveler-has-to-cancel-a-reservation-am-I-still-charged-a-booking-fee). Host penalties and an exceptional-disruption policy are separate controls. [Host cancellation](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/Partner-Cancellation-Fee-Policy), [extenuating circumstances](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Extenuating-Circumstances-Policy).
- **Booking.com/OpenTable:** Policies are provider-specific and disclosed during booking and confirmation, rather than a universal platform tier. OpenTable distinguishes deposits, credit-card guarantees, and prepaid bookings; a timely deposit cancellation can refund automatically, while restaurant policies govern later/manual refunds. [Booking.com terms](https://www.booking.com/content/terms.en-gb.html), [OpenTable policies](https://help.opentable.com/s/article/What-are-cancellation-no-show-and-deposit-policies?language=en_US).
- **ClassPass:** Generally allows cancellation without penalty until 12 hours before a reservation, with earlier cutoffs communicated where applicable. Late cancellation/no-show may produce a fee or forfeited credits. This is a membership/credit model, not a justification for imposing a universal 12-hour commercial-kitchen rule. [Cancellation policy](https://help.classpass.com/hc/en-us/articles/207942743-What-is-the-reservation-cancellation-policy).
- **Turo/Getaround:** Turo differentiates refundable/nonrefundable trips, grace periods, late cancellation, and full refunds when the host cancels or fails to provide the car. It emails refund amounts and retains original booking-time grace-period reference after changes. Getaround's international standard policy uses a 48-hour full-refund window and a short post-payment grace period, with a partial-refund stage; vehicle categories can differ. Its cancellation flow previews the amount. [Turo timing](https://help.turo.com/refund-timing-rkyN44gEc), [Getaround policy](https://fr.getaround.com/help/articles/77ae2d7092b0/intl).
- **The Food Corridor:** Kitchen managers can set late-cancellation windows and flat/percentage fees; cancellations after billing may generate credits. This supports location-specific commercial rules, but does not establish how LocalCooks should refund a Stripe Checkout payment. [Kitchen billing/cancellation](https://help.thefoodcorridor.com/en/articles/1413981-how-are-food-businesses-charged).

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

- Preserve each location's existing agreed windows, but translate them into a consistent policy structure: cutoff basis, refund percentages by line item, fee treatment, no-show definition, and examples. Replace vague “strict” labels with exact rules. For new policy presets, consider **Flexible: full until 24 hours, then zero** and **Standard: full until 72 hours, half from 72 to 24 hours, then zero**. These are proposed kitchen-specific starting points, not copied industry norms; validate capacity economics with managers before adoption. Longer exclusive daily blocks may need a separately reviewed firm option.
- Display the **actual cancellation deadline** before acceptance and in confirmation: date, time, kitchen timezone, and refund amount. Define whether hours mean elapsed hours or local calendar days, and what happens exactly at the cutoff. Cancellation becomes effective when the platform receives the valid request, not when a manager reads an email.
- Snapshot a policy ID/version **and its substantive content** with the accepted quote, amounts, consent timestamp, timezone, and computed deadlines. Preserve it for requests and confirmed bookings; later listing edits must not retroactively change it. An amendment needs an explicit version, price/refund difference, and acceptance record. Specify whether original grace periods remain anchored to the original booking.
- Show a preview separating kitchen time, equipment, storage, cleaning, guest transaction fee, tax, amount retained, and amount returned. Refunding “50%” without defining the base is insufficient. Decide service-fee refundability explicitly. For manager cancellation, unavailable premises, or a LocalCooks payment/inventory error, recommend full return of guest-paid charges including platform fees, subject to mandatory law.
- For partial use, distinguish elapsed use from unused sessions, recurring storage, and consumable add-ons. Refund tax according to the reviewed tax treatment, not an unexamined percentage of every tax line. A cancellation charge can have its own tax consequences.
- Use a documented exception path for unsafe kitchens, mandatory closure, serious disruptions, and verified emergencies. A small team can review cases manually with reason/evidence and an audit trail. Specify guest relief separately from whether the manager receives a penalty waiver. Do not copy a large platform's personal-emergency exclusions without considering kitchen food-safety obligations.
- Require manager cancellation to send an explanation and refund status; record repeated avoidable cancellations and restrict booking privileges when appropriate. Begin with education and measured consequences. Do not introduce automatic punitive fines without reviewed contracts and proportionality.
- A future damage claim remains a separate process; it must not silently delay an otherwise owed cancellation refund or redefine the original cancellation policy.

### Later at scale

Add three or more tested presets, long-block/recurring variants, automated exception routing, manager reliability controls, alternate-kitchen assistance, and self-service amendments. Keep historical policies inspectable. Use observed cancellation and rebooking rates to set windows, rather than assuming accommodation's 30/60/90-day policies fit hourly kitchens.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

Confirm cancellation/no-show fees, penalties, partial-use accounting, taxes on forfeited deposits or cancellation payments, and enforceability across provinces. Determine required remedies for unsafe premises, host failure, force majeure, and statutory rights that override contracts. Confirm how amended bookings acquire new terms without removing accrued rights.

## 3. Fees, Canadian taxes, supplier identity, and invoices

### What top companies do

**Price disclosure:** Airbnb made total pricing with fees standard globally in April 2025, generally before taxes, with jurisdiction-specific tax display. Vrbo describes displayed booking totals that include required fees. Neither is a reason to hide a LocalCooks mandatory checkout fee until the last screen. [Airbnb total-price announcement](https://news.airbnb.com/total-price-display-is-now-standard-globally), [Vrbo service-fee disclosure](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/what-is-the-service-fee).

**Canadian law:** The June 2022 Competition Act amendments explicitly addressed unattainable advertised prices caused by obligatory charges. The June 2024 amendments clarify that the government-charge exception concerns charges imposed on the **purchaser**, such as sales tax. A merchant's business expense passed through as an admin/processing fee is not thereby an exempt government charge. Variable charges can still create misleading representations; variability is not blanket permission to hide mandatory fees. [Competition Bureau 2022 guide](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/guide-2022-amendments-competition-act), [June 2024 guide](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/guide-june-2024-amendments-competition-act).

**Marketplace GST/HST scope:** Canada's digital-economy GST/HST measures took effect July 1, 2021. CRA's accommodation branch concerns a place of **lodging**, generally rented to an individual for less than one month and more than $20 per night. Registered accommodation suppliers continue collecting their own GST/HST; qualifying platforms can be responsible for supplies by nonregistered suppliers. **Inference requiring professional confirmation:** ordinary commercial-kitchen use is not lodging, so that accommodation rule does not establish a universal obligation for every platform facilitating rental of space. Normal GST/HST, agency, and taxable commercial-property rules may nevertheless apply. [Digital-economy overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst.html), [accommodation rules](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/platform-based-accommodation.html), [commercial real-property guidance; older publication, not a current rate source](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/19-4-1/commercial-real-property-sales-rentals.html).

**Rates and documentation:** Current CRA guidance lists NL HST at 15%; Nova Scotia is now 14%, illustrating why expansion cannot use a single Atlantic rate. Place-of-supply rules determine the applicable province and can differ by supply. CRA requires adequate documentary support for input tax credits. Booking.com for Business directs accommodation customers to the property for its invoice, distinguishing platform confirmation/payment documentation from the supplier's tax invoice. [Current GST/HST rates/place of supply](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate.html), [ITC documentation](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/input-tax-credit.html), [Booking.com invoice guidance](https://help.business.booking.com/hc/en-us/articles/14862570197268-How-can-I-get-my-invoice).

**Separate reporting regime:** CRA's digital-platform reporting rules include commercial and residential immovable-property rentals as relevant activities. Possible seller-information/reporting duties are a different question from whether LocalCooks must collect GST/HST. [CRA platform-reporting guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/programs/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/compliance/reporting-rules-digital-platforms/guidance-on-reporting-rules.html).

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

- Show an attainable total for selected dates/duration and quantities, including mandatory charges. Present a clear breakdown and, where determinable, the tax-inclusive total early. Before dates are selected, explain the unit and unavoidable fee basis; a misleading low “from” price is not repaired solely at checkout.
- Keep optional equipment/storage genuinely optional and actively selected. If an add-on is required for the intended use, include it in that applicable quote. Show equipment quantities, storage duration/billing period, minimum booking duration, currency CAD, and rounding consistently.
- Separate the **manager's commission deduction** from any **guest-paid transaction fee**. Do not add the commission twice. A zero admin fee must be supported without false minimum charges or unexplained line items. Identify the taxable base for each fee through accountant-approved rules.
- Treat manager-configured tax as reviewed supplier configuration, not proof of compliance. Record legal supplier, GST/HST registration status/number where applicable, location, tax basis, rate/effective date, tax amount, and commission/fee tax treatment. A nonregistrant must not simply be allowed to label an arbitrary surcharge “HST”; any platform collection obligation needs its own legal basis.
- Produce a downloadable receipt/invoice with booking/reference number, issue date, supplier legal/business name and address, purchaser business details where required, service location and local dates, item descriptions/quantities, taxable amounts, rates/tax totals, currency, relevant registration numbers, and payment/refund references. Identify LocalCooks' capacity as platform/agent or supplier consistently with the contracts. A Stripe payment receipt alone does not determine who supplied the kitchen rental.
- If there are multiple suppliers, distinguish the kitchen/equipment/storage supply from the platform fee, either through valid agent-issued documentation or separate invoices as advised. Issue proper refund/credit documentation. Reconcile gross collected, tax owed, commission, processing cost, manager payable, and refunds independently.
- Obtain an accountant's decision on the 2021 digital-economy branches and on digital-platform seller reporting **now**; these should not be deferred merely because the marketplace is small.

### Later at scale

Add effective-dated, supply-specific tax rules for each province, including separate QST/PST questions rather than treating GST/HST as the entire Canadian sales-tax system. Consider Stripe Tax or another engine only after establishing who is legally liable for each supply. Automate registration verification workflows, compliant credit notes, accountant exports, and any applicable platform reporting.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

- Is LocalCooks agent, principal/reseller, or a deemed supplier for any particular activity? Who collects/remits tax on kitchen time, equipment, storage, commission, guest fees, deposits, cancellations, and damage recovery?
- Does any 2021 digital-economy branch apply to this precise business model? Is a CRA written interpretation/ruling appropriate? Does a kitchen licence differ from real-property rental for this purpose?
- Which registration thresholds, place-of-supply rules, provincial sales taxes, reporting obligations, and invoice/ITC requirements apply? Confirm seller-reporting scope, exclusions, onboarding data, filing dates, and privacy obligations.
- How should tax be calculated and rounded on partial refunds, retained fees, bundles, and zero-fee bookings? What contractual authority permits LocalCooks to issue a manager's invoice?

## 4. Stripe Connect, financial reconciliation, and saved-card damage charges

### What top companies do

The public marketplace policies above establish payment and refund behaviour; they **do not reveal those companies' internal Stripe charge types**. The following standards come from Stripe's official documentation, not inferred Airbnb/Peerspace architecture.

**Charge-type choice:** Direct charges place the charge on the connected account and suit some merchant/SaaS models. Destination charges place it on the platform and automatically transfer funds to one connected account. Separate charges and transfers decouple platform collection from one or more transfers, including later transfers, at the cost of extra reconciliation. `on_behalf_of` identifies the settlement merchant and changes payment characteristics such as descriptor and settlement country; it does not independently decide legal supplier or tax liability. [Connect charge types](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges).

**Refund and commission mechanics:** With separate charges and transfers, the platform normally retains its fee by transferring less; the standard flow does not use destination-charge `application_fee_amount`. Refunding the charge does **not** automatically reverse a separate transfer. A `transfer_group` associates records; it does not reserve funds. `source_transaction` links a transfer to the charge and its availability. For destination charges, refunding normally leaves transferred funds with the connected account unless `reverse_transfer` is requested; `refund_application_fee` controls application-fee refund and must be coordinated with transfer reversal. [Separate charges/transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers), [destination charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/destination-charges?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted).

**Liability:** For indirect charges, disputes and associated fees debit the platform, including when `on_behalf_of` is used. Direct charges debit the connected account, but ultimate negative-balance responsibility depends on configuration. Recovering funds by transfer reversal can fail or be constrained by insufficient balance. [Disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes).

**Events and retries:** Account webhooks receive platform activity, including indirect charges. Connected-account webhooks receive relevant connected activity, including direct charges, Accounts v1 updates, and payouts; use the event's account context. Accounts v2 event scoping differs. Delivery can be duplicated and out of order; verify signatures using the raw body, record event IDs, acknowledge promptly after durable acceptance, and process asynchronously. [Connect webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/webhooks), [webhook delivery/security](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks). Stripe idempotency protects retried API operations but v1 keys may be pruned after at least 24 hours; permanent business-operation deduplication remains necessary. API/webhook schema versions need explicit management. [Idempotency](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests), [versioning](https://docs.stripe.com/api/versioning).

**Confirmation:** Checkout fulfilment must rely on verified payment state and webhooks, not the success redirect alone. A completed session is not proof that every asynchronous payment has succeeded; manual authorization also is not capture. [Checkout fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted).

**Payouts:** Balance availability, platform-to-manager transfer, and manager-to-bank payout are different operations. Payout scheduling does not change when a charge becomes available. Stripe's manual-payout page currently specifies a 90-day limit for countries other than the US and Thailand, therefore including Canada; it explicitly says Stripe does not provide escrow services. That is not a universal entitlement to hold every booking's funds indefinitely. [Payout scheduling](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manage-payout-schedule), [manual payouts/limits](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manual-payouts), [connected-account payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/payouts-connected-accounts).

**Saving cards and charging later:** A SetupIntent or Checkout `setup_future_usage=off_session` can prepare a card for later use. Stripe requires permission and terms specifying the intended use, timing/frequency, and how the amount is determined, with agreement retained. Technical card saving is not consent to arbitrary damage charges. Later off-session attempts can require customer authentication. PSD2 SCA is a European regime, not a general Canadian statutory requirement; issuer authentication and applicable cross-border rules still matter. [SetupIntents/permission](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/setup-intents), [saving during Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/save-during-payment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted), [later payment/consent](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/save-and-reuse?payment-ui=elements), [SCA](https://docs.stripe.com/strong-customer-authentication).

**Testing:** Stripe Test Clocks simulate **Billing**, especially subscriptions; they are not a general clock for expiring one-off kitchen bookings. [Billing Test Clocks](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/testing/test-clocks).

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

1. **Retain separate charges/transfers unless a specific requirement justifies migration.** Delaying manager transfer until use is complete can justify the current choice. For a new single-manager booking collected and transferred immediately, destination charges may be simpler. Direct charges would change payment ownership and account-level handling; do not switch merely to imitate an unverified competitor architecture.
2. **Define settlement merchant and legal supplier separately.** Review whether `on_behalf_of` is appropriate and whether the manager has the required capabilities. Align descriptor, receipts, terms, and account configuration; do not toggle it as a substitute for tax analysis.
3. **Maintain one financial ledger per booking.** Link Checkout session, PaymentIntent/charge, refunds, transfers, reversals, tax, commission, processing cost, and manager payable. Track refund requested/pending/succeeded/failed separately from cancellation. A cancelled booking can still require a pending refund or failed transfer-recovery action.
4. **Coordinate money movements safely.** Refund only the entitled amount; reverse the corresponding manager transfer if it already occurred, or reduce/cancel the unpaid manager payable. Record the sequence, failures, and compensating actions because these operations are not one atomic transaction. Do not refund the customer twice while retrying transfer recovery. Tax/platform-fee allocation follows the policy and accounting decision. Destination-charge refund flags must not be copied blindly into the separate-transfer implementation.
5. **Make events repeat-safe and account-aware.** Use distinct platform/Connect endpoints or carefully configured equivalent scopes; track account context, live/test mode, and signed payloads. Persist events durably before acknowledging, deduplicate delivery IDs, and guard each financial business action with a stable operation ID. Do not deduplicate all `object ID + event type` updates permanently: the same object can legitimately update repeatedly. Fetch current Stripe state when delivery order is ambiguous; transitions must not regress paid bookings to unpaid after a stale event.
6. **Pin and review versions.** Pin SDK and API versions and the webhook endpoint schema version deliberately; keep representative webhook fixtures and an upgrade procedure. Do not silently adopt the newest API release during booking work.
7. **Confirm only when the product's conditions are met.** Check approval, current resource ownership, and server-verified successful capture/payment for a paid-confirmation model. Handle async failures and expired sessions explicitly. Send confirmation once, despite concurrent webhook and redirect handling.
8. **Publish a realistic manager payment promise.** Proposed startup target: release the manager transfer within one business day after completed use and eligibility checks, then explain Stripe availability and the manager's bank-payout schedule separately. Confirm this is feasible for the account setup before promising it. Keep sufficient platform liquidity for refunds/disputes; review long-advance reservations against applicable holding limits rather than calling the funds escrow.
9. **Require a reviewed damage-claim process before off-session charging.** Obtain affirmative, damage-specific consent covering permitted reasons, amount calculation/cap if applicable, timing, advance notice, and challenge route. Retain accepted terms/version, timestamps, and evidence. Save the payment method to the correct Customer through supported Stripe flows. Human-review the claim, notify the renter of evidence and proposed amount, provide the agreed response period, and create a separate traceable charge/receipt only when authorized. A generic “save for future purchases” checkbox or saved card token is insufficient. If authentication is required, return the renter to an authenticated on-session payment flow; do not repeatedly force an off-session charge.
10. **Verify meaningful failure cases before launch.** Sandbox cards/events and an application-controlled test clock should cover duplicate/out-of-order events; approve-versus-expire races; payment after inventory release; partial/full refunds before/after transfer; insufficient reversal balance; disconnected/restricted manager accounts; payout failure; off-session authentication/decline; and DST boundaries. No Stripe account testing was performed during this research.

### Later at scale

Add automated reconciliation, payout failure recovery, liquidity/risk monitoring, dispute evidence workflows, configurable release schedules, and cross-border settlement review. Consider deposits or scheduled future charges for long lead times. Billing Test Clocks become useful if storage is sold as Stripe Billing subscriptions. Automate damage collection only after the consent, evidence, adjudication, and appeal process works reliably.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

Confirm settlement-merchant choice versus contractual supplier, merchant/account negative-balance responsibility, payout holding limits for the exact fund flow, reserves, and applicable Canadian payments-regulatory obligations. Review damage-charge enforceability, mandate/card-network requirements, notice, appeal rights, tax treatment, evidence retention, privacy, and cross-border authentication requirements. Obtain Stripe confirmation for any unusually long holding period or extended authorization; a network feature is not legal approval.

## 5. Transactional email, notifications, mobile design, and CASL

### What top companies do

Airbnb sends booking confirmation and can supplement it with SMS/push; Peerspace alerts hosts to requests; Vrbo emails acceptance/confirmation. Those are event-specific communications, not merely receipts. [Airbnb reservation status](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/234), [Peerspace request notifications](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10119524-what-happens-when-i-receive-a-booking-request), [Vrbo request lifecycle](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-accept-a-booking-request).

Resy documents confirmation, change, and cancellation emails with dynamic reservation details. Its standard SMS schedule includes a previous-day confirmation, a same-day follow-up if unanswered, and near-arrival reminders, with venue/region variations. Its emergency-closure instructions keep cancellation emails enabled by default and prohibit promotional content in customized cancellation messages. These demonstrate lifecycle coverage; they do not establish a universal reminder time for kitchens. Turo sends refund notifications with the amount and receipt. [Resy emails](https://helpdesk.resy.com/en_us/guest-communications-emails-HyZVfvmId), [Resy SMS](https://helpdesk.resy.com/en_us/standard-resy-automated-sms-content-S110vQIu), [closure communications](https://helpdesk.resy.com/en_us/how-to-handle-closures-H1fdbwXU_), [Turo refunds](https://help.turo.com/refund-timing-rkyN44gEc).

For email production, Mailchimp documents 600px template conventions, responsive mobile behaviour, HTML/plain-text alternatives, and accessible content. Litmus documents client-specific dark-mode behaviour. A **44×44 CSS-pixel** tap target is WCAG's enhanced **AAA** criterion; WCAG 2.2 AA's minimum criterion is generally 24×24 with exceptions, so 44px is a sensible LocalCooks design target rather than a universal AA legal rule. [Template images/width](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-template-images/), [mobile behaviour](https://mailchimp.com/help/campaign-behavior-on-mobile/), [HTML email/plain-text alternative](https://mailchimp.com/help/about-html-email/), [email accessibility](https://mailchimp.com/help/accessibility-in-email-marketing/), [dark-mode guidance](https://www.litmus.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-dark-mode-for-email-marketers), [W3C enhanced targets](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced), [minimum targets](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum).

**CASL nuance:** Section 6(6)(b) removes the consent requirement for a message that solely facilitates/completes/confirms a commercial transaction previously agreed to. It does **not** itself remove section 6(2)'s identification, contact, and unsubscribe requirements. Separate full exemptions may apply under section 6(5) or regulations; classify the actual message rather than calling all transactional mail exempt. Promotional purpose can arise from content and links. [CASL, sections 1 and 6](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-1.html), [CRTC guidance/FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm).

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

Use a small set of reusable templates triggered by durable booking/payment events:

- **Request received:** immediate renter acknowledgement and manager review request. State “awaiting approval,” full local dates/timezone, total, exact response deadline, pending-payment treatment, and next actor. Do not imply kitchen access is confirmed.
- **Decision reminders:** proposed manager reminders at 12 hours and two hours before request expiry, with an authenticated review link. These are LocalCooks choices, not copied company requirements. Suppress reminders once a decision occurs.
- **Approved/payment due:** renter payment link, locked total and terms, expiry, and “not confirmed until payment succeeds.” Manager sees awaiting-payment status. Skip this separate step only for a documented authorize-then-capture flow.
- **Confirmed:** both parties receive booking ID, kitchen/address, local start/end dates and timezone, access/onboarding instructions, selected equipment/storage, payment status, cancellation deadline, support link, and receipt access. Include a calendar attachment/download.
- **Declined/withdrawn/expired or payment failure:** send the relevant result to both affected parties; distinguish no charge, released authorization, refund initiated, and failed payment. Give the actionable next step without a fake refund promise.
- **Changed:** show material before/after differences, revised cost and cancellation implications, and required acceptance. Update the calendar only when the change is effective.
- **Pre-arrival:** proposed 24-hour reminder where useful, with shorter operational reminders opt-in/configured by the location. Include preparation/access requirements. Cancel stale scheduled messages after cancellation/reschedule; tours need their own labelled appointment reminders.
- **Cancellation/refund:** immediate cancellation acknowledgement with reason category, line-item refund calculation, retained amount, and status. Follow with the actual payment/refund outcome where it changes. Tell managers how their payable was adjusted; do not expose their payout details to renters.
- **Completion and manager settlement:** final receipt/adjustments where needed; manager transfer/payout notices distinguish release from bank receipt. A review request, upsell, or post-tour “book now” campaign needs its own CASL classification and consent basis.
- **Damage claim:** notice of evidence and proposed amount, response deadline, dispute/support route, then charge/authentication/outcome and separate receipt. A payment-success email must not be the renter's first notice of a claim.

Each message should answer **what happened, what happens next, who must act, by when, and how to get help**. Show the kitchen's local date/time **and timezone on every time-bearing field**, including request/payment/cancellation deadlines. For reminders, date/time clarity matters more than an ambiguous “tomorrow.”

Use a responsive single column with **600px maximum content width**, readable text, generous spacing, and preferably 44px minimum primary tap targets. Keep essential information as text rather than image artwork; provide alt text, descriptive links, meaningful headings, and sufficient contrast. Test small screens, images disabled, screen readers, light/dark mode, and common Gmail/Outlook/Apple Mail clients. Supply `multipart/alternative` plain text. These are proposed acceptance criteria informed by the cited email guidance, not claims about every marketplace's HTML.

Use stable, authenticated deep links to the specific booking or tour, with a safe return path after login. GET links must open a review page rather than cancel, approve, or charge automatically; email scanners can prefetch links. Do not email sensitive card details or unnecessarily expose renter business documents. Provide an in-app record as well as email, log delivery failures, and make urgent failures visible to staff. Configure and verify sender authentication/deliverability through the chosen provider during implementation.

Use `.ics` events with stable `UID`, timestamps and revision `SEQUENCE`, location, booking link, and unambiguous UTC or `TZID`/timezone definitions. Keep tour and rental events distinct. Support updates and cancellation semantics using the relevant iCalendar/iTIP mechanism, and test actual client behaviour rather than assuming an imported download automatically updates itself. [iCalendar RFC 5545](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545), [scheduling/update/cancel protocol RFC 5546](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5546).

Keep promotional content out of lifecycle templates. Maintain separate marketing preferences and auditable consent, with compliant sender/contact/unsubscribe treatment for each message class. Design a legally reviewed route for necessary service communications when a user withdraws consent or changes preferences; do not assume “transactional” grants unlimited permission to email or text them.

### Later at scale

Add user/channel preferences, push/SMS for genuinely time-sensitive actions, bilingual/localized templates, delivery monitoring and replay tools, event-version checks for scheduled messages, calendar interoperability QA, and escalation when urgent notices fail. Add behavioural marketing only through a separately governed consent process.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

Classify each lifecycle, tour, abandoned-request, review, and promotional message under CASL, including who sends on whose behalf. Confirm which exemptions apply, footer/unsubscribe obligations, consent evidence, and the consequences of withdrawal for service messages and SMS. Review privacy/retention and safe disclosure of claims, health/safety information, and business documents. Confirm invoice attachments and credit-note requirements with the accountant.

## 6. Timezone handling, local deadlines, and DST

### What top companies do

Airbnb says cancellation deadlines follow listing local time; its guest cancellation help uses local check-in time, defaulting to 3:00 PM if none is specified. Peerspace uses space local time for policy periods. Vrbo expresses applicable cutoff days as 11:59 PM in the property timezone. Turo uses the vehicle's timezone for cancellation deadlines. These are different deadline conventions; there is no universal marketplace “midnight cutoff.” [Airbnb deadline help](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/169), [Peerspace policy](https://www.peerspace.com/legal/terms/cancellation-policy), [Vrbo policies](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-the-cancellation-policy-options), [Turo deadline/timing](https://help.turo.com/refund-timing-rkyN44gEc).

OpenTable documents restaurant policies shown at booking and in confirmation, but this research did **not** establish a universal public rule or exact wording for its timezone deadline presentation. Likewise, public marketplace help does not prove those companies internally store UTC plus IANA zones. That storage model below is a technical recommendation, not an invented company fact. [OpenTable policies](https://help.opentable.com/s/article/What-are-cancellation-no-show-and-deposit-policies?language=en_US).

IANA maintains timezone data as civil-time rules change. Canadian official-time guidance documents DST and Newfoundland's distinct zone; St. John's uses a half-hour offset, with standard and daylight offsets differing. A fixed “UTC−3:30” is not a year-round St. John's timezone. [IANA timezone database](https://www.iana.org/time-zones), [NRC Canadian time/DST](https://nrc.canada.ca/index.php/en/certifications-evaluations-standards/canadas-official-time/time-zones-daylight-saving-time), [NRC zone/offset map](https://nrc.canada.ca/sites/default/files/2025-04/metro-time-zone-maps-side-by-side-e.pdf).

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

- Give every location an **IANA zone**, initially `America/St_Johns` where appropriate. Store resolved start/end and deadline instants in UTC, together with the location zone and the accepted local civil-time inputs/offsets needed for historical explanation. Keep timezone data current. Do not infer the kitchen zone from the renter's device, postal abbreviation, or fixed UTC offset.
- Display kitchen local time first everywhere: listing, picker, review screen, status page, email/SMS, cancellation preview, receipt, tour, and calendar description. Show an optional viewer-local conversion only with a separate label. Operational/audit tools should expose the UTC instant and location zone as well.
- A proposed display example is: **“Cancel before 3:00 PM on October 12, 2026 — St. John's time (NDT, UTC−02:30; America/St_Johns).”** This is an illustrative LocalCooks design, not a quotation of an Airbnb/OpenTable reservation. The instant is 17:30 UTC. A compact public label may use the city and correct seasonal abbreviation; retain the IANA identifier in details.
- Use both dates for bookings spanning midnight. A daily booking must define its local start/end and access hours; “one day” does not inherently mean 24 elapsed hours. Storage must define monthly or other billing periods and access conditions.
- Reject nonexistent spring-forward local times, or ask the user to choose a valid time. For repeated fall-back times, require an explicit occurrence/offset rather than silently picking one. Decide whether hourly pricing bills actual elapsed time or defined wall-clock slots, and explain the result before consent. Verify these conversions against a timezone library during implementation.
- Define cancellation rules precisely: **24 elapsed hours before the start** and **one local calendar day before** are different across DST. Compute and snapshot the actual UTC deadline using the accepted rule; compare server timestamps. Specify whether action at exactly the cutoff qualifies. Do not recalculate a historical booking using a manager's newly edited policy or a newly edited listing timezone.
- Enforce the same conversion and formatting for TOUR. A renter arriving for a tour in their device timezone must not receive a contradictory rental deadline in fixed Newfoundland standard time.
- Test Newfoundland half-hour offsets, both DST transitions, cross-midnight bookings, users in another province, repeated/nonexistent local inputs, and exactly-at-deadline cancellation. For future civil-time rule changes, preserve the original accepted record and use an explicit re-resolution/notification process when the intended local session time must change its UTC mapping.

### Later at scale

Add timezone-aware recurring wall-clock schedules, occurrence-specific exceptions, location-zone validation, and timezone-database upgrade checks. Re-resolve recurring future occurrences according to the intended local schedule rather than adding fixed 24-hour UTC increments across DST. Provide multi-zone manager dashboards without changing the location-local contract presentation.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

Confirm how deadline inclusivity, hourly duration through DST, daily access periods, future legal time changes, and time-zone-labelled contract notices should be expressed. Review which date determines tax/service recognition for cross-midnight and recurring use. Legal review cannot replace timezone conversion testing.

## 7. Trust, reassurance, support, and TOUR integration

### What top companies do

Airbnb exposes reservation statuses and explains pending versus confirmed. Peerspace shows average initial response time and a response rate based on replies within 24 hours; a fast message reply is not necessarily a booking decision. Its support help provides a route when a host is unresponsive. OpenTable allows management through reservation email links and distinguishes restaurant-controlled matters from platform help. [Airbnb statuses](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/234), [Peerspace response metrics](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10119527-what-is-my-response-time-and-response-rate), [unresponsive-host help](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10119106-what-do-i-do-if-a-host-is-unresponsive), [OpenTable restaurant/help responsibilities](https://help.opentable.com/s/article/What-can-a-restaurant-help-me-with?language=en_US).

The Kitchen Door's recommendation to view first and One World KitchenShare's onboarding process address a kitchen-specific trust issue: suitability and readiness matter before paid use. Getaround documents pickup/return photographs and practical checklists; the transferable lesson is recorded condition/evidence, not importing vehicle insurance promises into kitchens. [Kitchen Door terms](https://www.thekitchendoor.com/tos), [KitchenShare onboarding](https://www.kitchenshare.net/faq), [Getaround owner preparation](https://fr.getaround.com/help/articles/f52e7280ff82/intl).

These are observed reassurance mechanisms. This research did not find controlled evidence proving that any particular badge or email increases LocalCooks conversion; outcomes should be measured rather than asserted.

### Recommended for LocalCooks now (startup stage)

- Put a clear timeline on one persistent booking page: request submitted, manager decision, payment, confirmation, use, completion. Include current status, next actor, exact deadline, what happens on timeout, and correct payment wording. Show cancellation/refund/claim progress alongside the relevant booking without concealing either state.
- Before requesting, show factual suitability information: kitchen address/area, access hours, equipment quantities, storage type/capacity, cleaning responsibilities, permitted activities, orientation/documents required, accessibility/contact information, and current images. State the source and limits of any verified inspection, permit, insurance, or compliance claim. Do not invent a “verified safe” badge or guarantee unverified coverage.
- Make **Book a tour** a visible option where useful, link confirmed/completed tours to the location's booking page, and show outstanding onboarding requirements. “Tour completed” should reassure the renter about familiarity while remaining distinct from manager approval, licensing compliance, and resource availability.
- Show a truthful response promise first. Add response-time badges only after enough real observations, with a defined metric and sample threshold. Keep first-message reply time separate from decision time; do not advertise “responds within an hour” based on an automated acknowledgement.
- Expose relevant support at request, payment, cancellation, and access stages. Publish staffed hours and realistic expected response times; do not promise 24/7 assistance without staffing. Provide a clear urgent safety/access route and explain which matters the manager resolves versus LocalCooks.
- Provide a durable receipt and line-item refund explanation; show refund initiated versus actually succeeded, with bank timing caveats. Let users retrieve historical accepted policies and communications from the booking page.
- For equipment/damage, record agreed pre/post-use condition and incident evidence proportionately, with access controls and consent. Give both parties a fair way to correct errors and challenge a claim. Photos support evidence but do not automatically prove fault.
- Keep manager/renter messaging tied to the correct location and booking/tour. Disclose contact details only where operationally necessary and legally appropriate. Avoid a tour's conversation becoming an ambiguous promise of rental availability.

### Later at scale

Add verified-use reviews, measured host reliability, rebooking assistance, documented guarantees only when funded and contractually supported, fraud controls, insurance integration where appropriate, and operational support coverage. Measure time to decision, request expiry, payment abandonment, avoidable manager cancellations, refund completion, access failures, and tour-to-booking outcomes; use those findings to improve the flow.

### Open legal/accounting questions to confirm with a professional

Review inspection/verification claims, manager representations, food-business eligibility, liability allocation, insurance scope, tours and on-site injury risks, photo/document retention, reviews, and dispute fairness. Confirm what support/remedies LocalCooks must provide and which guarantees would create obligations beyond its present capacity. Ensure marketing badges do not make misleading claims.

## Prioritized checklist of standards LocalCooks booking must meet

### P0 — required for a trustworthy paid-booking launch

- [ ] **Unambiguous state model:** request, approval, payment due/authorized, paid confirmation, use, cancellation, and refund status cannot contradict each other. A tour is a separately identified appointment.
- [ ] **One documented pending-payment model:** disclose no charge, authorization, or immediate charge accurately; enforce manager/payment/authorization/session expiry and notify both parties. Proposed review SLA: 24 elapsed hours.
- [ ] **No double booking or double payment:** kitchen/equipment/storage capacity is checked atomically; duplicate events, stale approvals, and late payments have explicit repeat-safe outcomes.
- [ ] **Accepted terms retained:** immutable quote, substantive policy/version, fee/tax breakdown, local zone, computed deadlines, and consent records are retrievable; material changes require new acceptance.
- [ ] **Attainable pricing:** mandatory fees included early, add-ons opt-in, selected-duration total and CAD currency clear, zero admin fees supported, final tax-inclusive amount explicit.
- [ ] **Tax/supplier determination:** accountant documents who collects/remits each tax, registration basis, NL 15% applicability, invoice identity, cancellation/damage treatment, and whether digital-platform reporting applies. Do not assume all space rental falls under the 2021 lodging-platform rules.
- [ ] **Predictable cancellation:** exact local deadline and refund preview; fee/add-on/tax treatment defined; manager/platform failure handled; emergency/unsafe-kitchen review and refund-status tracking available.
- [ ] **Money reconciled:** each charge/refund/transfer/reversal/commission/tax/manager payable linked; refund cannot leave an unexplained manager payment or cause duplicate recovery.
- [ ] **Reliable Stripe events:** signatures verified, durable asynchronous processing, account/live-mode scope correct, business idempotency enforced, API/webhook versions pinned, confirmation based on verified successful payment rather than redirect alone.
- [ ] **Controlled manager settlement:** truthful release/bank timing and sufficient refund/dispute liquidity; long-lead bookings reviewed against actual holding limits; no escrow claim.
- [ ] **Damage-charge consent and process:** explicit reviewed authorization, retained terms, evidence, advance notice, human decision, appeal route, authentication fallback, and separate receipt before automated off-session collection is enabled.
- [ ] **Local-time consistency:** UTC instants plus IANA zone; every displayed time includes timezone; DST/midnight/deadline edge cases verified; TOUR uses the same standard.
- [ ] **Complete accessible lifecycle notifications:** request/decision/expiry/payment/confirmation/change/cancellation/refund/access messages, clear next action/support, single-column mobile layout, plain-text alternative, tested dark mode, calendar attachment/link, safe deep links, and suppressed stale reminders.
- [ ] **CASL classification approved:** operational versus promotional message purposes, sender/on-behalf-of identity, applicable consent/exemptions, unsubscribe treatment, and marketing separation recorded.
- [ ] **Honest reassurance:** current timeline, real response expectations, staffed support, correct receipts, factual kitchen suitability, and tour/onboarding requirements; no invented safety or insurance guarantees.

### P1 — before multi-province or multi-timezone expansion

- [ ] Effective-dated province/supply-specific GST/HST and any QST/PST rules, supplier registration checks, accountant exports, and applicable seller reporting.
- [ ] Additional IANA zones, viewer-local secondary display, recurring/DST behaviour, and future time-rule-change procedures tested.
- [ ] Standard location cancellation presets and long-block/storage variants reviewed for legal rights, manager economics, fees, and taxes.
- [ ] Reconciliation alerts, restricted-account/payout failure recovery, dispute evidence, and long-advance payment arrangements operational.
- [ ] TOUR and rental permissions/resource calendars remain linked but independently correct; sufficient delivery/support monitoring covers increased volume.

### P2 — as volume and operational maturity justify it

- [ ] Instant Book for qualified renters and reliable managers, measured response/reliability badges, waitlists, and recurring reservations.
- [ ] Automated exception routing, alternative-kitchen assistance, expanded staffed support, and funded/contracted guarantees if introduced.
- [ ] Additional notification channels/localization, calendar interoperability, and consent-governed marketing automation.
- [ ] Advanced risk/reserve management, tax automation, monthly-storage Billing simulations, and carefully governed damage-claim automation.
